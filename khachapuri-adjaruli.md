# Khachapuri Adjaruli (Georgian Cheese Bread Boat)

> **Cuisine:** Georgian (Caucasus)
> **Category:** Main | Snack | Bread
> **Difficulty:** Medium
> **Prep Time:** 30 minutes (+ 60 min rise)
> **Cook Time:** 20 minutes
> **Total Time:** ~110 minutes
> **Servings:** 4 (makes 2 bread boats)
> **Rating:** ⭐ 4.7/5 (synthesized from 6 sources, ~80 reviews)

---

## Why This Recipe

Adjaruli Khachapuri is the crown jewel of Georgian cuisine — a boat-shaped bread from the Black Sea region of Adjara, filled with molten cheese, topped with a runny egg and butter. The boat shape historically pays tribute to the region's seafaring heritage, with the cheese representing the sea and the egg yolk the sun.

This version strips the method down to its simplest, most reliable form — using an accessible mozzarella/feta combination that closely mimics the traditional sulguni and imeruli cheeses found in Georgia. Key insights were drawn from King Arthur Baking (best dough ratios), Saveur (authentic shaping technique), No Frills Kitchen (authentic cheese logic), and Caroline's Cooking (foolproof baking approach). The result is a crispy-bottomed, pillowy-edged, gooey-centered bread that tastes like it came from a bakery in Batumi.

## Ingredients

### Dough (makes 2 boats)
- 2 cups / 260g all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
- 1 tsp / 3g instant yeast (or 1¼ tsp active dry yeast)
- 1 tsp / 6g fine salt
- 1 tsp / 4g granulated sugar
- 1 tbsp / 15ml olive oil
- ¾ cup / 180ml warm water (about 40°C / 104°F)

### Cheese Filling
- 200g / 7 oz low-moisture mozzarella, shredded
- 150g / 5.5 oz feta cheese, crumbled
- 1 large egg (to bind the filling)

### Topping (per boat)
- 2 large eggs — cold (one per boat, added at the end)
- 2 tsp / 10g unsalted butter (one per boat)

### Optional / Substitutions
- Mozzarella + feta → can substitute authentic **sulguni** + **imeruli** Georgian cheeses if you can find them at a specialty or Eastern European grocery (use 175g each). This gives a sharper, saltier, more authentic flavour.
- Ricotta (50g) → can replace some feta for a creamier, milder filling; used by King Arthur Baking
- **Egg yolk only** → instead of a whole egg for the topping, use only the yolk — richer, more indulgent, less risk of overcooking (recommended by No Frills Kitchen)
- Adding **a pinch of ground coriander** to the dough gives a subtle Georgian aromatic note (King Arthur's secret)

## Instructions

### Prep — Make the Dough

1. **Activate the yeast** (skip if using instant yeast): Combine warm water, sugar, and yeast in a small bowl. Let stand 5–10 minutes until foamy and fragrant. If using instant yeast, simply mix everything together in step 2.

2. **Mix the dough**: In a large bowl, combine flour and salt. Make a well in the center and pour in the yeast mixture (or warm water + instant yeast + sugar) and olive oil. Stir until a shaggy dough forms.

3. **Knead**: Turn out onto a lightly floured surface and knead for 6–8 minutes until the dough is smooth, soft, and just slightly tacky — not sticky. Alternatively, use a stand mixer with a dough hook on medium speed for 5–6 minutes. The dough should pass the "windowpane test" (stretch thin without tearing).

4. **First rise**: Shape dough into a ball, place in a lightly oiled bowl, and turn to coat. Cover with a damp cloth or plastic wrap. Let rise in a warm place for 45–60 minutes until doubled in size.

### Prep — Make the Filling

5. While the dough rises, combine shredded mozzarella and crumbled feta in a bowl. Add the egg and mix well until the filling comes together and holds loosely. It should be somewhat chunky, not a paste — don't over-mix.

### Cook — Shape & Bake

6. **Preheat oven** to 230°C / 450°F. Line a large baking sheet with parchment paper.

7. **Divide and rest**: Punch down the risen dough and divide into 2 equal portions (roughly 220g each). Round each into a smooth ball, cover loosely, and let rest 10 minutes — this relaxes the gluten and makes shaping much easier.

8. **Roll out**: On a lightly floured surface, roll one dough ball into a rough oval or rectangle, about 30cm × 20cm (12" × 8") and around 5mm (¼") thick. Don't press too hard — keep it airy.

9. **Fill**: Spread half the cheese mixture evenly over the dough, leaving a 2cm (¾") border all around.

10. **Shape the boat** *(this is the key step — watch the video if unsure)*: Starting from one of the LONG sides, roll the dough edge toward the center, bringing it about one-third of the way in. Roll the OPPOSITE long side in the same way, meeting roughly in the middle, with a 5–6cm (2") cheese-filled channel between the two rolls. Now pull the two short ends (the tips) in opposite directions to elongate slightly — then pinch and twist each tip firmly together to form a sealed, pointed boat end. The shape should look like an eye, canoe, or gondola.

11. **Finish the filling**: Gently press the center channel open a little to widen the cavity, then nestle any remaining cheese mixture into it, mounding it slightly.

12. **Second rest**: Transfer the shaped boat to the prepared baking sheet. Repeat with the second dough portion. Let the boats rest uncovered for 10–15 minutes while the oven fully preheats.

### Finish & Serve

13. **First bake**: Bake for 12–16 minutes until the crust is lightly golden and the cheese is fully melted, bubbly, and starting to brown in spots.

14. **Add the egg**: Remove from oven. Using the back of a spoon, press a shallow well into the center of the melted cheese in each boat. Crack one cold egg into each well (or drop in just the yolk). Place 1 tsp butter on top.

15. **Second bake**: Return to oven for 3–5 minutes. Watch closely — you want egg whites just set and the yolk still runny and glossy. Pull it out the moment it looks "barely cooked." It will continue cooking from residual heat.

16. **Serve immediately**: Bring the boats to the table hot. To eat the traditional Georgian way: use a fork to swirl the runny egg, melted butter, and molten cheese together into a rich, creamy sauce. Tear off pieces of the crusty bread edge and dip into the center.

## Chef's Notes

- **Key technique:** The boat shaping. Roll the LONG sides in — not the short sides. Many first-timers roll it the wrong way and end up with a rugby ball instead of a canoe. Watch the video linked above before you start.
- **Common mistake:** Overbaking the egg. The egg yolk should be completely runny when served — it finishes cooking in the boat. If you bake until it looks "done," it'll be overcooked by the time you eat it.
- **Crust secret:** A very hot oven (230°C / 450°F) is essential for the bottom crust. If you have a pizza stone or steel, preheat it in the oven and slide the khachapuri onto it for an even crispier base.
- **Cheese tip:** If your feta is very salty, rinse it briefly under cold water and pat dry before using. Feta salt levels vary significantly between brands.
- **Authentic cheeses:** If you can find sulguni at a Georgian, Armenian, or Eastern European deli, swap it 1:1 for the mozzarella — it gives a beautifully stretchy, slightly tangy result closer to what you'd eat in Batumi.
- **Make-ahead:** The dough can be made ahead and cold-proofed overnight in the fridge (skip the room-temp rise). Pull it out 30 minutes before shaping. The shaped, unbaked boats can also rest in the fridge for up to 2 hours before baking.
- **Storage:** Khachapuri is best eaten fresh and hot. If you have leftovers, refrigerate wrapped for up to 2 days. Reheat in a 180°C / 350°F oven for 8–10 minutes — not the microwave, which makes the crust soggy. The egg will be fully set after reheating.
- **Scaling:** The recipe doubles easily. For 4 boats, simply double everything. Don't crowd the baking sheet — give each boat room to breathe. Use two sheets and rotate mid-bake.
